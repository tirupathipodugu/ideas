How the numbers work

An illustration from a simple energy-balance model, not a prediction or medical advice.

  • Weight follows a simplified Hall model (The Lancet, 2011): in the long run about 1 kg for every 24 kcal a day over or under what you burn, about half of it in the first year.
  • That change splits into fat and lean tissue with the Forbes curve: the leaner you are, the more of it is lean tissue.
  • Fat goes where it really goes: the waist moves about 1 cm per kg (more in men), hips and thighs more in women, arms and neck a little, calves hardly at all. Turn side-on and the belly comes forward more than it spreads. Only the waist figure is measured in studies; the others are estimates.
  • Strength training adds muscle on a slowing beginner curve (about 0.3 kg a month at first at 4× a week, less for women), mostly to shoulders, chest, arms and thighs.
  • The body stops changing beyond +15 kg, never goes leaner than −8 kg, and never below a healthy weight (BMI 18.5).
  • Only you move. The background stays where it is; where a leaner body uncovers it, the gap is filled from the background next to you.
